    Rendering Molecules in Blender

    A workbook for structural biologists who can already make a figure in ChimeraX and want what Blender adds: materials, light and geometry you control yourself.

    10 parts, 109 chapters and 194 exercises, written for Blender 5.2 LTS and the Molecular Nodes add-on.

    How the parts feed each other. Start at any node whose input you already have.

    1. Material recipes for molecular renders Settings tables for named looks, from slime and frosted glass to matte clay, all on one Principled BSDF. 9 chapters, 13 exercises
    2. Designing a look from scratch Five questions that turn a texture you can picture into settings, asked in the order light meets the surface. 8 chapters, 12 exercises
    3. Lighting, from zero to designing your own setups Direction, softness and contrast first, then the named setups, then inventing a setup of your own. 9 chapters, 19 exercises
    4. Shaders: the whole menu, from zero Every shader node Blender offers, what feeds one, the render engines, and flat, stylized looks for figures. 12 chapters, 25 exercises
    5. Geometry Nodes, from zero Node trees, attributes and fields, then volumes and SDFs, ending with a sticky-slime map and local-resolution coloring. 9 chapters, 21 exercises
    6. Geometry Nodes for art The same nodes fed by formulas instead of files: instances, variation, curves and motion in three worked pieces. 9 chapters, 13 exercises
    7. Molecular Nodes as a second ChimeraX Selections as boolean fields: each Select node, when to use it, and the plain Geometry Nodes inside it. 7 chapters, 12 exercises
    8. Handling objects and modeling Selecting, placing and parenting objects, Edit Mode, modifiers, curves and the camera, with a cartoon house to practice on. 10 chapters, 12 exercises
    9. Art direction: the whole picture Seven decisions made together for one sentence, from form and stage to palette and air, then four complete looks to build, take apart and outgrow. 14 chapters, 45 exercises
    10. Composition and layout: a step-by-step tutorial Arranging things so a viewer sees what matters first: hierarchy, grouping, the grid, the frame and the eye path, applied to figures, posters, renders and manga pages. 22 chapters, 22 exercises

    How to use it

    Keep the page open beside Blender; it stays readable in a narrow window. Each chapter opens with what you will be able to do, explains the idea, then sets exercises that say what you should see when it works.

    The questions that close a chapter keep their answers folded until you ask. Ticking an exercise as done is remembered in this browser and nowhere else.

    Where to start

    • You have never moved an object in Blender: Objects and modeling, Chapters 1 to 3.
    • Your structure is on screen and looks like wet plastic: Recipes, which opens with why.
    • You want to change what is drawn, not how it is shaded: Geometry Nodes, from zero, then Molecular Nodes.
    • Every control is in place and the picture still looks regular: Art direction, Chapters 1 to 3.
    • Your figure, poster or slide is crowded or hard to follow: Composition and layout, Chapters 0 to 11.

    What you need

    • Blender 5.2 LTS. Menus and node names move between versions; when a step does not match your screen, press Shift + A and type the node's name.
    • The Molecular Nodes add-on.
    • A model or a map of your own. A PDB entry does as well as unpublished data.

    How it was made

    Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, wrote the text in working sessions with Jiaze Liu, who did the exercises in Blender and reported what broke. Not every chapter has been checked that way yet.
